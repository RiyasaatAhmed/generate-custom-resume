# Proof of work and interview preparation

For a high-priority role, first select an existing relevant case study, demo, repository,
publication, or talk. Explain exactly what it demonstrates. A link alone does not prove
an adoption or business-impact claim. Check that the artifact is shareable.

If a missing demonstration materially weakens the application, suggest a small time-boxed
sample. Examples include a streaming interface with failure states, a large-data rendering
benchmark, or a synthetic-data workflow. Do not describe proposed work as completed,
label synthetic data and benchmark conditions, and never promise production impact.
Building or publishing a substantial new project is a separate task.

A concise case study answers: problem, constraints, candidate contribution, alternatives,
choice and tradeoffs, result, measurement, limitations, and artifact link. Private work
may be described at an approved level without exposing code, client details, or data.

When interview preparation is requested, save `interview-prep.md`. For each selected
achievement, develop a STAR outline from the same evidence. Include personal ownership,
team contributions, measurement context, important tradeoffs, what did not work, and
what the candidate would do differently only when those facts are known. Mark missing
facts as rehearsal questions rather than writing a fictional story.

Identify likely questions from the posting and the candidate's claims. Ask the candidate
to explain complex performance or impact statements in plain language. Flag uncertainty
before an interview instead of making every story sound certain. The preparation is
successful when the candidate can explain the work consistently across all documents.
